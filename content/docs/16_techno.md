## Basics

### Core terms
- **Technopreneurship** = entrepreneurship in a **technology-intensive context**; the merging of **technology prowess and entrepreneurial skills**.
- **Technopreneur** (Daniel Mankani, 2003) = an entrepreneur who uses **cutting-edge technology** to develop a new business.
- **Entrepreneur** (Kuratko & Hodgetts, 2007) = an innovator who **recognizes opportunities**, **adds value**, **assumes the risks** of the competitive marketplace and **realizes the rewards**.
- **Technology Developers** = **Inventors** (create unique technology). **Technology Users** = **Innovators** (apply a technology to a new market need).
- **Technopreneurial process**: the **first step is Idea generation** (then screening, concept testing, business analysis, prototyping, test marketing, commercialization, monitoring).

### 4 types of entrepreneurs
| Type | Remember |
|---|---|
| **Innovative** | passion for discovering **new** products, ideas, processes; enjoys the challenge of risk |
| **Imitating** | **copies** products of innovators; imitates their technology, process and methods |
| **Fabian** | **skeptical** about change; waits for the outcome of released products before the next project |
| **Drone** | **reluctant to change**; happy with results even with **financial losses** |

### Innovation
- **Sustaining** innovation = **incremental**, a response to customer/market demands (Apple, Intel, Amazon).
- **Disruptive** innovation = makes products more **affordable and accessible**; popularized by **Clayton Christensen**; classic example: **cellphones vs. fixed-line telephony**.
- **Breakthrough** innovation = from **inside** a company, pushes something to the next level and opens **new markets**; example: **Apple iPhone**.
- **Basic research** innovation = starts with the **discovery of a new phenomenon**; seeks to understand how nature and people are put together.
- **ABC analysis** (rough idea selection): **A** = necessary, go on · **B** = uncertain, discuss · **C** = **rejected**, no longer considered.
- Detailed analysis criteria include **customer benefit**, differentiation potential, technical and economic feasibility.
- **Elevator pitch** = a **short, persuasive presentation** of an idea.

### Market research & validation
- **Survey** = list of questions by phone, paper or online. **Observation** = watching how a subject uses a product. **In-depth interview** = one-on-one with probing questions. (Fourth tool: focus group.)
- **Market validation** = finding out whether your product is of **interest to a target market**, usually **before significant investment**.
- **Alpha testing** = **internal employees**, staged setting, remove bugs before outsiders see it. **Beta testing** = a **limited group of real external users** told to find problems.

### Business Model Canvas (BMC)
- Proposed by **Alexander Osterwalder**; 9 blocks.

| Block | Remember |
|---|---|
| **Customer segments** | who the customers are; what they think, see, feel, do; **multi-sided market** = two sides (CNN.com: readers + advertisers) |
| **Value propositions** | the value a company **promises to deliver** to customers |
| **Channels** | phases: **Awareness** (word of mouth, social media, TV, flyers) → Evaluation → **Purchase** (self-checkout, credit card, COD) → Delivery → After sales |
| **Revenue streams** | **cash generated** from each customer segment |
| **Key resources** | physical, **intellectual** (patents, brands, copyrights, customer lists), human, financial |
| **Key partnerships** | what the company will **not do** so it can focus on key activities; **coopetition** = alliance between competitors; **joint venture** = two firms create a new business together |
| **Cost structure** | **all costs** of operating the model; **value-driven** (more value in the product) vs **cost-driven** (minimize costs) |

### Intellectual property (5 types: copyright, patent, trade secret, trademark, industrial design)
- **Copyright** = literary and artistic works: **computer programs (source code)**, databases, maps, technical drawings, advertisements.
- **Patent** = **exclusive right over an invention**. **Utility** patent = process, article, machine, composition of matter. **Design** patent = original, new, **ornamental design** of a manufactured product.
- **Trade secret** = practice/process **not known outside the company** that gives an economic advantage.
- **Trademark** = distinguishes a seller's products by **brand name or logo**. **Service mark** = protects **services**. **Collective mark** = owned by an organization/association. **Certification mark** = shows adherence to local regulations.
- **Industrial design** = **ornamental/aesthetic** aspect of an article (3D shape or 2D patterns).

### Money & planning
- **Angel investors** = individuals making **high-risk** investments in **early-stage** ventures.
- **Private equity (PE) firms** = invest in **relatively mature** ventures with a track record, **1–5 year** horizon.
- **Strategic investors** = usually **larger companies in the same industry**. **Public funding agencies** = fund ventures for economic, environmental, cultural or social-policy goals. **Banks** = **lowest cost of capital**.
- **Crowdfunding** = raising money from **a large number of people, usually online**.
- **Financial planning** = determine **capital requirements** and **capital structure**. **ROI** = **return on investment**.
- **Balance sheet** = lists assets and liabilities. **Break-even analysis** = sales level where **total revenue equals total costs**.
- **Business plan** = document summarizing operational and financial objectives with detailed plans and budgets; its **executive summary** briefly summarizes the whole plan.

## Entrepreneurship vs. technopreneurship
- **Entrepreneurship** = embarking on a new business or reviving an existing one by pooling resources to exploit new-found opportunities. It is **"creating the new and destroying the old"** → new **knowledge, products, processes, services, markets, business models, raw materials**.
- **Entrepreneur** (Kuratko & Hodgetts, 2007): an innovator/developer who **recognizes and seizes opportunities**, converts them into workable/marketable ideas, **adds value** through time, effort, money or skills, **assumes the risks** of the competitive marketplace, and **realizes the rewards**.

### 4 types of entrepreneurs
| Type | Key idea |
|---|---|
| **Innovative** | passionate about discovering/developing **new** products, ideas, processes; risk-taker, enjoys challenges |
| **Imitating** | **copies** products of the innovative entrepreneur; same features/specs; imitates the technology, process and methods pioneered by others |
| **Fabian** | **skeptical** about change; waits to see the result/outcome of released products before moving; **not a risk-taker** |
| **Drone** | **reluctant to change**, happy with results **even with financial losses**; not eager to change anything |

### Technopreneur
- **Daniel Mankani (2003):** an entrepreneur who uses **cutting-edge technology** to develop a new business.
- **Sarimah Hanim & Abd. Rashid (2008):** an entrepreneur involved in **technological changes** in producing goods/services for his organization.
- Entrepreneurs who use **"technology" as the driving factor** in transforming resources into goods and services.
- **Technopreneurship** = entrepreneurship in a **technology-intensive context**; the **merging of technology prowess and entrepreneurial talent and skills**. A **technology-based enterprise** derives a **competitive advantage from direct or indirect use of technology**.

### 2 types of technopreneurs
| Type | Also called | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Technology Developers** | **INVENTORS** | develop unique technology capable of driving a new business |
| **Technology Users** | **INNOVATORS** | see a new technology and understand how it can meet a **new market need** |

### Slide examples
- **Apple iPod** – innovation: **160 GB, 1.8-inch hard disk drive** (smaller than the average 3.5-inch drive of 40 GB).
- **UPS (United Parcel Service)** – **one-day delivery anywhere in the world**; sorting/delivery facility processing up to **1,000,000,000 parcels a year**.
- Technopreneurship is **not limited to high-tech stuff**: an **internet café is NOT a technology-based enterprise** even though it uses computers in operations.

### Shared traits (entrepreneur & technopreneur)
Determines risk & has courage to take it · independent and self-confident yet knows where to get help · likes a challenge · hardworking, sticks with a project · not easily discouraged · robust, energetic, handles stress · strong sense of self-worth · positive thinker who doesn't dwell on setbacks · often has a close friend/relative who owns a business.

### The two processes

## Innovation & idea generation
- **Innovation** = creation of something that **improves the way we live our lives** (Barack Obama quote). Per **Joerg Gemuenden**: a **process** involving multiple activities by multiple actors, during which new combinations of means and ends are developed, implemented and transferred.

### 4 types of innovation
| Type | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Sustaining** (a.k.a. **routine** innovation) | **incremental**; response to customer/market demands or tech improvements; based on customer feedback. Examples: **Apple, Intel, Amazon** |
| **Disruptive** | popularized by **Clayton Christensen** in *The Innovator's Solution* (follow-up to ***The Innovator's Dilemma***, **1997**); turns expensive/sophisticated products into **affordable, accessible** ones for a broader population → **displaces established competitors**. Classic example: **cellphones vs. fixed-line telephony** |
| **Breakthrough** | from **inside** a company; pushes something to the **next level**; opens **new markets** or changes how customers interact with the industry. Example: **Apple iPhone** (slide says "January 29, 2007" — in reality unveiled **Jan 9** and released **Jun 29, 2007**; answer with the slide's wording if asked) |
| **Basic research** | begins with the **discovery of some new phenomenon**; goal = collect information about **how nature and people are put together**; key driver of innovation |

### 10 principles for sustainable innovation
1. **Inspire** – a leader who can inspire others
2. **No risk, no innovation** – technopreneurs are risk takers
3. **New product process** – structured process (idea generation, etc.)
4. **Ownership** – business leader owns the new products/innovation
5. **Value creation** – new products must solve **real customer problems**
6. **Accountability** – team & leaders have full responsibility
7. **Training and coaching** – hiring, staffing training, continuous coaching
8. **Idea management** – value customers and sales members' input (brainstorming)
9. **Observe and measure** – track results (**ROI**) when releasing new products
10. **Net result and reward** – if ROI is achieved, **incentives** for employees

### 10 idea-presentation styles
Storytelling · Musical · Video · Photo-only · Immersive · Question · Demonstrative · Humorous · Animated · Props.

### Idea selection – 3 steps
1. **Rough selection – "ABC analysis"** (separate the chaff from the wheat): **A-ideas** = absolutely necessary, go on; **B-ideas** = uncertain, discussed and decided together; **C-ideas** = no value, rejected.
2. **Fine selection** – **"dot gluing"**: each participant gets **5 sticky points** to distribute; count → ranking. If **fewer than 10 ideas**, use the **rating method**: each participant gives each idea **0–5 points**; sum → ranking.
3. **Detailed analysis** – qualitative evaluation on criteria: **customer benefit · differentiation potential & competitive factor · technical and economic feasibility**. A canvas such as **Osterwalder's** can be used for new business models.

## Market research & validation
- **Market research** = systematically gathering data about people/companies (a market) and analyzing it to understand what the group needs; conducted to **justify the needs of customers**.

### 4 primary market-research tools
| Tool | Note |
|---|---|
| **Surveys** | list of questions via phone, in person, paper/card, or online software |
| **Focus groups** | group with a common characteristic (age, hobby, buying habits); typically **8–12 people** with a **moderator** |
| **Observation** | researcher watches how a subject interacts with a product |
| **In-depth interviews** | **one-on-one** with probing questions |

- **Market validation** = determining whether your product is of **interest to a target market**; a series of **customer interviews**, almost always **before significant investment** (source: HBS Online).

### 5 steps to determine market validation
1. Write down **goals, assumptions and hypotheses**
2. Assess **market size and share** (example: **Casper** mattresses)
3. Research **search volume** of related terms (example: "best mattress for lower back pain sufferers" = **240 monthly searches**, per Moz)
4. Conduct **customer validation interviews**
5. **Test your product or service** – **alpha** and **beta** testing

| **Alpha testing** | **Beta testing** |
|---|---|
| **internal employees**, staged setting | **limited group of real, external users** told to find problems |
| eliminate bugs before outside users see it | may be open to the public with an "unfinished" notice |

### Consumer decision-making process (5 stages)
**Problem/need recognition → Information search → Evaluation of alternatives → Selection stage → Evaluation of decision.**
Customers' needs are the **foundation** of creating a product; an innovative leader must be **resourceful** (info search), provide **different choices**, **select the best idea** after research, and "whatever the company decided is considered the best idea."

## Business Model Canvas
- Used by startups as a guiding tool to think of better solutions, generate innovation ideas and market products/services. **Proposed by Alexander Osterwalder.** (Slides also follow **Alex Cowan**'s explanation.)

### The 9 building blocks
| # | Block | Guiding question / key points |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Segments** | Who are the customers? What do they **think, see, feel, do**? Single- vs **multi-sided** market (e.g., **CNN.com**: readers + advertisers); **personas**; problems, needs, habits, current alternatives |
| 2 | **Value Propositions** | Why do customers buy? Value a company **promises to deliver**. Examples: **newness, high performance, customization, design, brand/status, price, cost reduction, risk reduction, convenience** |
| 3 | **Channels** | How are propositions **promoted, sold, delivered**? 5 phases: **Awareness → Evaluation → Purchase → Delivery → After-sales** |
| 4 | **Customer Relationships** | How you interact through the customer "journey." 3 benefits of good relationships: **retention, loyalty, satisfaction** |
| 5 | **Revenue Streams** | cash generated from each customer segment |
| 6 | **Key Activities** | most important things the company must do |
| 7 | **Key Resources** | strategic assets needed to compete |
| 8 | **Key Partnerships** | what the company *won't* do so it can focus on key activities |
| 9 | **Cost Structure** | all costs of operating the model |

**Channel phases:** Awareness (word of mouth, social media, newspaper, TV, flyers) · Evaluation (surveys, interviews, reviews) · Purchase (self-checkout, credit card, COD) · Delivery (over-the-counter or door-to-door) · After sales (call center, return policy, customer assistance).

**Ways to generate revenue (7):** sale of physical product · usage fee · subscription fee · lending/renting/leasing · brokerage fees · advertising · volume and unit selling.

**Key activities:** **R&D** (new product research/development, existing product updates, quality checks, innovation) · **Production** (product & process selection, capacity, planning, control, quality/cost control, inventory, machine maintenance) · **Marketing** (strategy, market research, product development, communications, sales support, events) · **Sales & customer services** (handling problems, assisting sales, clerical tasks).

**Key resources (4):** **Physical** (equipment, inventory, buildings, plants, distribution networks) · **Intellectual** (brand, patents, IP, copyrights, partnerships, customer lists/knowledge) · **Human** · **Financial** (cash, lines of credit, stock-option plans). The BMC proposes **3 core business types: product, scope, infrastructure** (slide wording).

**Key partnerships:** strategic alliances between **non-competitors** · **coopetition** (alliance between competitors) · **joint ventures** to develop new businesses · **buyer–supplier** relationships. Slide example: **Google** — **97% of revenue from advertising**.

**Cost structure:** **value-driven** (create more value in the product, not lowest cost) vs. **cost-driven** (minimize costs).

## Intellectual property rights
- **Intellectual property** = creations of the mind: inventions, literary & artistic works, designs, symbols, names and images used in commerce.
- **5 types:** **Copyright · Patents · Trade secrets · Trademarks · Industrial design.**

| Type | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Copyright** (author's right) | rights over literary & artistic works: books, music, paintings, sculpture, films, **computer programs, databases**, ads, maps, technical drawings. Rights: **reproduce, prepare derivative works, distribute copies, perform publicly, display publicly**. Application: **fill out form → attach a copy of the work → pay filing fee at the IPO** |
| **Patent** | exclusive right for an **invention** (a product or process offering a new way of doing something / new technical solution). Types: **Utility** (process, article, machine, composition of matter) · **Design** (original, new, ornamental design) · **Plant** (new reproducible plant). Basic requirements: **novel, inventive, industrially applicable**. Application: request form with descriptions & drawings → **published in the IPO Gazette** (anyone may contest) → pay filing fees **Php 1,800 / 3,600** |
| **Trade secret** | practice/process generally **not known outside the company**; gives economic advantage; (1) not public, (2) secrecy gives economic benefit, (3) secrecy is actively protected |
| **Trademark** | distinguishes a manufacturer's/seller's products & services in the marketplace; a corporate symbol / mark of quality |
| **Industrial design** | **ornamental or aesthetic** aspect of an article: **3D** (shape, surface) or **2D** (patterns, lines, color) |

**4 types of marks:** **Trademarks** (identify products) · **Service marks** (protect non-product items, i.e., services) · **Collective marks** (owned by organizations/associations) · **Certification marks** (registered to ascertain adherence to local regulations).

## Financial plan & funding
- **Financial plan** = comprehensive overview of financial goals and steps to achieve them; clear vision of operating income & expenses to tell whether the business will profit.
- **Objectives (4):** (a) determine **capital requirements** (short & long term) · (b) determine **capital structure** (kind & proportion of capital) · (c) frame **financial policies** (cash control, lending, borrowing) · (d) ensure scarce funds are maximally used at least cost for maximum ROI.
- **Importance (6):** adequate funds · balance of inflow/outflow (stability) · suppliers/investors prefer firms that plan · growth & expansion programs · reduce uncertainties from market trends · stability & profitability.

### 6 types of investors
| Investor | Note |
|---|---|
| **Angel investors** | individuals making **high-risk investments in early-stage** ventures |
| **Public funding agencies** | mandate to fund ventures for economic, environmental, cultural or social-policy objectives; good for early stage |
| **Venture capital companies** | established to invest in **high-risk** ventures with potentially **high returns** |
| **Private equity (PE) firms** | invest in **relatively mature** ventures with track record; **1–5 years** time frame |
| **Strategic investors** | defined by intent; usually **larger companies in the same industry** |
| **Banks** | offer the **lowest cost of capital** |

**Raising capital for startups (5):** funding your own idea · **crowdfunding** · friends & family · taking a loan · entering competitions.

**Components of a small-business financial plan (8):** income statement · balance sheet · personnel plan · business ratios · cash-flow projection · income projections · assets & liabilities · **break-even analysis**.

## Business plan, Lean Canvas & pitch · finals
- **Business plan** = document summarizing the **operational and financial objectives** with detailed plans and budgets showing how objectives will be realized.
- **Importance (4):** raise money · make sound decisions · identify potential weaknesses · communicate your idea with stakeholders.
- **Benefits (10):** see the whole business · strategic focus · set priorities · manage change · develop accountability · manage cash · strategic alignment · milestones · metrics · realistic regular reminders.
- **Outline (7):** 1 **Executive summary** (purpose, problem, problem analysis, results of analysis, recommendations) · 2 Company description · 3 Product or service · 4 Market analysis · 5 Strategy and implementation · 6 Management team · 7 Financial plan.

### Lean Canvas – 9 blocks (slide order)
1 **Problem** · 2 **Customer Segments** · 3 **Unique Value Proposition** · 4 **Solution** · 5 **Key Activity** · 6 **Channels** · 7 **Cost Structure** · 8 **Revenue Streams** · 9 **Unfair Advantage**.
> ⚠️ Ash Maurya's original Lean Canvas has **Key Metrics** in place of "Key Activity." The slide says **Key Activity** — use the slide's wording.

### Elevator pitch
- A quick presentation delivered on the fly; **concise**, usually **30 seconds to 2 minutes**.
- **5-step pitch (turns strangers into clients):** 1 **Introduce yourself** (name, company, service) · 2 **Identify the problem** your company solves · 3 **Announce your promise** · 4 **Offer proof and a plan** · 5 **Know when to stop and listen**.
- **3 essential components:** 1 **Stimulate interest** (most important) · 2 **Transition that interest** (address pain/fear/guilt; logic matters) · 3 **Share a vision** (value proposition backed by the **100/20 Rule**; say exactly what you want, why, and what you'll give in return).
- **Final tips (4):** take your time · make it conversational · avoid niche words and phrases · express confidence.
